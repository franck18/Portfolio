01

Before starting

The need and my role

The need

Prevent a single gateway or uplink from making the VLANs unavailable, while keeping a configuration that can be observed and tested from Cisco equipment.

My role

I designed the topology, configured the VLANs and SVI interfaces, set up HSRP and LACP port-channels, diagnosed trunk inconsistencies and carried out failover tests.

Keep a stable default gateway for devices in every VLAN.
Match the LACP and trunk settings at both ends.
Keep layer 2 loops under control with STP.
Validate continuity without claiming an unmeasured convergence time.
02

Why I chose this approach

The chosen solution

HSRP for the gateway, LACP for the links

HSRP provides a virtual IP address independent of the active switch. LACP groups compatible links in a port-channel and keeps traffic flowing when one member fails. The two mechanisms address different and complementary failure points.

Two layer 3 switches provide routing between VLANs.
Each VLAN uses an HSRP virtual address as its default gateway.
Priorities and the preempt option determine the expected active switch.
Links to the access switch are grouped into LACP port-channels.
The port-channels carry the permitted VLANs in trunk mode.
STP continues to protect the layer 2 domain.

04

Checking that it works

How I checked my work

Tests performed

  • Checked Active and Standby states with `show standby`.
  • Checked the SU state and grouped interfaces with `show etherchannel summary`.
  • Disabled one port-channel member during test traffic.
  • Made the active HSRP device unavailable and checked takeover by the standby device.

Observed results

  • Active and standby HSRP roles are visible and match the configured priorities.
  • LACP interfaces appear grouped in an operational port-channel.
  • When one member cable is removed, the second link continues to carry traffic with no visible interruption during the test.
  • The standby gateway becomes active during the failover scenario.
05

Looking back

Limits and improvements

Security and operation

  • Redundancy improves availability but does not replace inter-VLAN filtering or control-plane protection.
  • Consistent trunk settings reduce unintended VLAN exposure errors.
  • Real operations would also require monitoring of role changes and LACP member status.

What I would improve

  • Convergence time and packet loss were not measured precisely.
  • The laboratory does not cover redundant power supplies, providers or application services.
  • STP protections, DHCP snooping and port security still need to be included in a complete production scenario.
